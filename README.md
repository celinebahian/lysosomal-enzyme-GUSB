# Overview

GUSB (beta-glucuronidase) is an enzyme found in lysosomes, which are structures inside cells that break down and recycle waste materials.

- **What it breaks down:** GUSB breaks down certain complex carbohydrates called glycosaminoglycans (GAGs), especially dermatan sulfate, heparan sulfate, and chondroitin sulfate.

- **NCBI Protein Record:** [NP_000172.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein/NP_000172.2)

- **Normal Function:** GUSB (beta-glucuronidase) breaks down glycosaminoglycans, including heparan sulfate, dermatan sulfate, and chondroitin sulfate, inside lysosomes by removing terminal beta-glucuronic acid residues.

## Review Questions & Answers

**1. What does your enzyme normally break down inside lysosomes?**

GUSB stands for beta-glucuronidase, a lysosomal enzyme that breaks down certain complex carbohydrates called glycosaminoglycans (GAGs). It helps degrade substances such as dermatan sulfate and heparan sulfate so that their components can be recycled by the cell.

**2. What is the total length of your protein, and which amino acids are most common?**

Based on the amino acid composition graph, the GUSB protein is approximately 651 amino acids long. The most common amino acid is leucine (L), with about 65 occurrences. Valine (V) is the second most common, with approximately 51 occurrences. Glycine (G) and serine (S) are also relatively common.

**3. How many potential N-glycosylation motifs did you find? Where are they located?**

I found 4 potential N-glycosylation motifs in the GUSB protein sequence. They are located at amino acid positions 173, 272, 420, and 631. The motifs are NNT, NGT, NVS, and NET, respectively. Each follows the N-X-S/T pattern, where X is not proline.

**4. Why can glycosylation matter for lysosomal proteins? Does finding a motif prove the protein is glycosylated?**

Glycosylation can help lysosomal proteins fold correctly, remain stable, and move through the cell to the lysosome. Some lysosomal enzymes receive a mannose-6-phosphate (M6P) tag that helps direct them to lysosomes. However, finding a potential N-glycosylation motif does not prove that the site actually has a sugar attached. It also does not confirm that the protein receives an M6P tag. Additional experimental evidence is needed to confirm glycosylation and enzyme activity.

**5. What could happen in the cell if your enzyme did not work properly?**

If GUSB does not work properly, glycosaminoglycans may accumulate inside lysosomes because they cannot be broken down normally. This can interfere with normal cell function and cause damage to tissues and organs. In humans, severe GUSB deficiency causes mucopolysaccharidosis type VII (Sly syndrome), a lysosomal storage disorder. However, the protein sequence analysis alone cannot determine whether the enzyme is functioning normally.
